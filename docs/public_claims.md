# SafeCross public-claims policy

## Why this exists

SafeCross concerns vulnerable road users and automated-driving research. Ambitious language can easily turn a simulation or benchmark result into an unsupported safety claim. This document defines what may be said publicly at each evidence level.

All public numbers must be generated by a checked-in evaluation command from saved result files. Demo overlays must read the same artifacts; no metric is typed manually into a video.

## Non-negotiable wording rules

Always distinguish:

- prediction from action;
- retrospective replay from closed-loop simulation;
- simulation from real-world testing;
- association from causation;
- a selected subset from an official full benchmark;
- development results from frozen-test results;
- absolute change from relative change.

Never claim:

- real-world collision reduction;
- validation on a Toyota, Woven, Waymo, or other production vehicle;
- production readiness;
- safety certification;
- state of the art without a current, like-for-like, independently verifiable benchmark result;
- commercial usability for artifacts restricted to non-commercial use;
- that public data represent the behaviour of a particular real autonomous-driving system.

Waymo explicitly states that its dataset mixes manually and autonomously driven operation and is not suitable for conclusions about a specific real vehicle: [Waymo FAQ](https://waymo.com/open/faq/).

## Evidence ladder

### Level 0: concept only

Evidence:

- architecture diagram;
- synthetic sketches;
- no executed benchmark.

Allowed:

> SafeCross is a planned research project exploring uncertainty-aware pedestrian and cyclist risk prediction.

Not allowed:

> SafeCross detects dangerous crossings early.

### Level 1: deterministic fixtures

Evidence:

- geometry and risk code passes synthetic unit scenarios;
- visual demo may use explicitly scripted trajectories.

Allowed:

> The prototype computes trajectory-conflict and warning-time measures on deterministic synthetic scenarios.

Required label:

> Scripted synthetic scenario; not a learned-model or safety result.

Not allowed:

> The AI prevented a collision.

### Level 2: offline public-data baseline

Evidence:

- official splits respected;
- at least one reproducible baseline;
- saved per-scenario predictions;
- no closed-loop vehicle response.

Allowed:

> On the Argoverse 2 validation subset defined in our manifest, the model improved [metric] from [baseline] to [result].

> The result is an offline motion-forecasting evaluation and does not measure vehicle safety.

Not allowed:

> SafeCross made driving safer.

### Level 3: frozen offline evaluation

Evidence:

- frozen leakage-safe split;
- multiple seeds;
- confidence intervals;
- calibration and subgroup results;
- external or held-out-region validation.

Allowed:

> Across [N] held-out interactions, SafeCross increased critical-mode recall by [absolute pp] at a matched false-warning rate. The paired 95% bootstrap interval was [low, high].

> This indicates stronger retrospective risk detection on the evaluated dataset, not real-world collision reduction.

Not allowed:

> SafeCross predicts pedestrian intent reliably in any city.

### Level 4: closed-loop simulation

Evidence:

- frozen CARLA scenario suite;
- at least 1,000 held-out episodes;
- matched scenario seeds across policies;
- at least two non-oracle baselines;
- three model seeds;
- collision/near-miss plus progress and comfort metrics;
- simulator version, configs, and failures published.

Allowed:

> In [N] held-out CARLA episodes, SafeCross reduced the simulated near-miss-or-collision rate from [A%] to [B%] versus [baseline], an absolute change of [X pp] and relative change of [Y%]. Route completion changed from [C%] to [D%], and the paired 95% confidence interval for the safety-endpoint difference was [low, high].

Required label on videos and posts:

> Closed-loop CARLA simulation. Not tested on a real vehicle.

Not allowed:

> SafeCross prevents pedestrian collisions.

Preferred:

> SafeCross reduced simulated conflict events in the evaluated CARLA scenario suite.

### Level 5: independent public benchmark

Evidence:

- official evaluation server or independent evaluator;
- exact track, date, and submission identifier;
- reproduction package where terms allow.

Allowed:

> The [method] submission achieved [metric/rank] on the [exact benchmark and version] leaderboard as of [date].

Not allowed:

> Industry-leading safety.

### Level 6: controlled physical research platform

Not part of the initial project. It would require appropriate institutional supervision, a closed course, safety driver/operator, risk assessment, and formal permission.

Even then, allowed claims are restricted to the tested platform and conditions. No public-road or production claim follows automatically.

## Public result template

Use this structure only after Level 4 is complete:

> **SafeCross: uncertainty-aware pedestrian and cyclist interaction prediction**
>
> I evaluated SafeCross on [N] held-out closed-loop CARLA episodes covering [scenario groups]. Against [strongest baseline], it changed the simulated near-miss-or-collision rate from [A%] to [B%] ([X percentage points], [Y% relative]; paired 95% CI [low, high]). Critical-event recall at the matched false-warning rate changed from [C%] to [D%], while route completion changed from [E%] to [F%].
>
> The release includes scenario manifests, per-episode results, baseline implementations, ablations, and failure cases.
>
> **Scope:** closed-loop simulation and public-data research only; not tested on a real vehicle and not evidence about any production automated-driving system.

## Demo-video policy

Every video must:

- display whether the scene is scripted, replayed, or closed loop;
- name the simulator and version;
- show identical scenario seeds for side-by-side comparisons;
- avoid hiding the baseline's favourable cases;
- link to the full scenario manifest and aggregate results;
- label oracle information if visualised;
- distinguish ground truth from prediction;
- preserve the real-time relationship between observation, decision, and action;
- avoid dramatic collision footage as a substitute for aggregate evaluation;
- include at least one false warning, miss, or failure case in the longer technical video.

Allowed overlay:

```text
Closed-loop CARLA simulation
Scenario seed: 1842
Model observation only
Decision: YIELD
Risk: 0.72
```

Disallowed overlay:

```text
COLLISION PREVENTED
100% SAFE
```

unless the first is narrowly described as a counterfactual within one scripted simulation and never generalized; the second is never acceptable.

## Dataset-licence claims

### Argoverse 2

Argoverse 2 is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Attribute it, preserve non-commercial/share-alike constraints for adapted material, and do not call an adapted release permissively licensed.

### Waymo Open Dataset

The [Waymo licence](https://waymo.com/open/terms/) is non-commercial and governs derivative models and generated datasets. Include its prescribed attribution. Do not call it open source, do not deploy artifacts in a vehicle or production system, and do not redistribute raw data outside the permitted registered-user route.

### CARLA

Audit code, content, and rendered-asset licences separately before release. A simulator's open-source code licence does not automatically establish unrestricted rights to every packaged asset or derivative render.

## Claim review checklist

Before publishing any post, portfolio page, résumé bullet, video, or repository release:

- [ ] Is the exact dataset/simulator version named?
- [ ] Is the evaluation split frozen and leakage-safe?
- [ ] Are `N`, numerator, and denominator stated?
- [ ] Is the baseline current and like-for-like?
- [ ] Are absolute and relative changes both correct?
- [ ] Is uncertainty shown?
- [ ] Are route progress and comfort included with safety metrics?
- [ ] Are pedestrians and cyclists reported separately?
- [ ] Are failure cases accessible?
- [ ] Is simulation/public-data scope visible near the claim?
- [ ] Are upstream licences and attribution satisfied?
- [ ] Could the wording be misread as a result on a real vehicle?

If the last answer is yes, rewrite it.
